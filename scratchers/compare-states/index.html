How we count

  • Prize counts, ticket prices and overall odds come straight from each state lottery's official data, pulled fresh every morning. Only games still in stores are counted.
  • Sales are our estimate, not the lottery's: prizes claimed times the game's overall odds, times the ticket price, averaged over the last four full weeks. Prizes count when they are claimed, so a busy week shows up a few days late.
  • A state needs history before we quote its sales: three weeks for the weekly figure, 12 for the week-by-week charts. Until then it shows a dash (today: Arizona, Connecticut, Louisiana, Minnesota, New Mexico and Washington DC). Its prize counts, odds and prize money are complete from the first day.
  • "Per resident" divides that estimate by the state's 2020 Census population. Visitors buy tickets too, so border and vacation states run a little high.
  • "Weekly for life" and other installment prizes count at their full advertised total. Free-ticket prizes count at the ticket's shelf price.
  • "Average overall odds" averages the chance of winning any prize across the games on sale. Overall odds include break-even prizes and describe a game on its first day.
  • These numbers describe whole lotteries. They do not predict what any one ticket will do. Nothing does.

Quick answers

Which state sells the most scratch-off tickets?

Of the 19 states SmartScratcher has enough history to measure, California sells the most: about $136M a week across 60 games, as of Nov 25, 2026, ahead of Florida at about $126.8M. That is our estimate from prizes claimed times each game's overall odds, not a figure from the lottery.

Which state spends the most on scratch-offs per person?

Massachusetts, at about $9.57 a week for every resident, by our count as of Nov 25, 2026. That is estimated weekly sales divided by the state's 2020 Census population, so it counts visitors' tickets too.

Which state has the best scratch-off odds?

By overall odds, Oklahoma: its 55 games on sale average 1 in 3.21, as of Nov 25, 2026. Overall odds count every prize, including the ones that only give your money back, and describe a game the day it launched. They are not a promise about any ticket, and the gap between two games in one state is usually wider than the gap between two states.

Which state has the most scratch-off prize money left?

Florida, with $5.1B in prizes still unclaimed across 75 games as of Nov 25, 2026. Across all 25 states it comes to $42.5 billion. For-life and installment prizes count at their full advertised total.

Which state has the most million-dollar scratch-off prizes left?

Massachusetts, with 207 prizes of $1 million or more still unclaimed as of Nov 25, 2026, by the lottery's own count. The 25 states together list 1,237.

Are scratch-off sales going up or down?

The 15 states with 12 full weeks of history sold about $810.8M in scratch-offs in the 7 days through Nov 25, 2026, by our count. The last four weeks are up 4% on the first four. The charts above show each state on its own; a state that has launched few new games lately tends to drift down as its games age.

How does SmartScratcher estimate sales?

Each lottery publishes how many prizes are still unclaimed at every prize level. The drop from one day to the next, times the game's overall odds, is our estimate of tickets sold, and tickets times the price is dollars. Prizes are counted when they are claimed, so a busy week shows up a few days late. It is a pace, not a sales report.

Does a higher-ranked state mean a better ticket?

No. These are facts about a whole lottery: how much it sells, what is printed on its tickets and what is still unclaimed. They say nothing about what one ticket will do, and no lottery game is a way to make money. What matters at the counter is the game: the SmartScratcher app scores every one from the prizes left today, by our math.

How fresh is this data?

Prize counts come straight from each state lottery's official data, last refreshed on Nov 25, 2026. SmartScratcher updates every morning, and this page is rebuilt with it.
