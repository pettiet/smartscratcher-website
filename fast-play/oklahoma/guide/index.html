How Fast Play works,
and what to look at

Fast Play looks simple: pay, print, find out. But the jackpots work in a way most players never hear about, and the counts on these pages show it.

1. It's a scratcher without the scratching

You buy a Fast Play ticket at the counter or a lottery machine. It prints on the spot and tells you right away if you won. No drawing, no waiting.

Pick a game and press print to get the next ticket from its set.

Here's the part that matters: the machine is not rolling dice. Every Fast Play game is a set number of tickets, with a set number of winners already decided. When you buy one, you get the next ticket from that set. It works just like a roll of scratchers, only printed instead of scratched.

That means the lottery knows how many prizes are left in every game, and it publishes those numbers. We read them twice a day.

2. The jackpot is shared, and it grows

Every Oklahoma Fast Play game has a jackpot that keeps growing until somebody wins it. All the games at the same price share one jackpot. Every ticket sold on any of them adds a little to it.

Today's example

Three $1 games share one jackpot: Blazin' Bucks Progressive, Double Play Progressive and Sugar Crush Progressive.

That jackpot started at $4,000 and has grown to $30,621. Each ticket sold adds a nickel.

When somebody wins the jackpot, it starts over at its starting amount. That happens for every game sharing it, all at once.

3. Same jackpot, different number of jackpot tickets

Games that share a jackpot pay the same jackpot, but each game has its own set of tickets, and only a few of those tickets are jackpot winners. As tickets sell, some games run low on jackpot tickets sooner than others.

Today's example

Casino Royale Progressive and Money Multiplier Progressive both play for the $10 jackpot of $125,383.

Casino Royale Progressive still has 3 of its 3 jackpot tickets in the pool. Money Multiplier Progressive is down to 2 of 3. Same price, same prize, different count.

Nothing on the ticket or the machine tells you this. It is the main reason we built these pages. What those counts mean for one ticket, as a score and an estimated value, is in the SmartScratcher app.

4. A game can run out of jackpot tickets

Each game only has a few jackpot tickets. When they have all been won, that game cannot win the jackpot anymore, even though it still shows the jackpot amount and still sells tickets.

Every Oklahoma Fast Play game still has at least one jackpot ticket today. We flag it on the game's page the moment one runs out.

5. What the counts can't show on their own

A big jackpot with only a few jackpot tickets left, a small jackpot with all of them still in the pool, a game nearly sold out: the counts tell you where each game stands, but not what one ticket is worth today. That takes a bit of math over every prize still unclaimed and every ticket still unsold.

That math is the SmartScratcher app's job. Pick Oklahoma in the app, flip the switch to Fast Play, and every game gets a SmartScratcher Score, an estimated ticket value and a place in the ranking, from the same counts you see here.

What to look at before you pick

  1. How big is the jackpot? Compare it with where it starts. The game pages show how it has grown since we began tracking it.
  2. Does the game still have jackpot tickets? If not, that jackpot is out of reach on that game, even though the ticket still shows it.
  3. How many prizes are left at every level? The full prize table on each game page shows what the game started with and what is still out there.
  4. Which one is worth it? The SmartScratcher app rolls all of it into a score, an estimated ticket value and a ranking.

What these numbers can't do

They can't tell you which ticket wins. Nobody can. The numbers show which games have more left in them, not what the next ticket will be.

A big jackpot is not closer to being won. A jackpot ticket can print at any time, whether the jackpot is small or huge.

Every game pays back less than it takes in. That is how a lottery works. A better game loses less on average. It is still a game, so play for fun and only with money you can spare.
