BETAMaryland Fast Play:
which ones are worth it

Fast Play tickets print right at the counter. No scratching, you know on the spot. Behind the scenes each game is a set pool of tickets with a set number of prizes, just like a scratcher. We track what's left in every pool and score each game from 0 to 100.

New to Fast Play? The jackpots work differently from scratchers, and it changes which game is worth picking. Read the 2-minute guide →

What to look at before you pick

  1. Does the game have a jackpot that grows? Some games do and some have a fixed top prize. Each jackpot belongs to one game. The bigger it has grown, the more each $1 pays back on average.
  2. What are the odds of winning the top prize? Every game has its own pool of tickets. The "Odds of winning it" column below shows each game's odds today.
  3. How many top prizes are left? Each game starts with a set number. Once they are won, that prize is gone from that game.
  4. How much of the value is tied up in the jackpot? Very few tickets win it. Each game's page shows what the regular prizes pay on their own.

How much of each $1 comes back

Longer bar, more money back on average. Each bar is an average across every ticket left in that game. It is not what one ticket pays.

Blue is the part of that average that sits in regular prizes. Gold is the part tied up in the jackpot. A lot of gold means much of the game's value depends on a few jackpot tickets.

A ticket that wins the jackpot pays the whole jackpot, far more than any bar here. Nearly every other ticket gets none of the gold part. Where a game has several jackpot tickets left, one is counted at today's jackpot and the rest at the amount the jackpot starts over at.

Fast Play or a scratcher?

Fast Play has the bigger top prize on $2, $5 and $20 tickets. Scratchers have the bigger top prize on $1, $10 and $30 tickets. Fast Play gives one ticket the better odds of winning the top prize on $1 and $10 tickets. Scratchers give one ticket the better odds of winning the top prize on $2, $3, $5, $20 and $30 tickets. The typical Fast Play game pays back more than the typical scratcher on $2 tickets. The typical scratcher pays back more than the typical Fast Play game on $3, $5, $10, $20 and $30 tickets. The two pay back about the same on $1 tickets.

Quick answers

What is Maryland Fast Play?

Fast Play games are sold at Maryland Lottery retailers and print from the lottery terminal when you buy one. There's nothing to scratch and no drawing to wait for. The ticket shows right away whether it's a winner.

Is each ticket just random?

No. Each game is a set pool of tickets with a set number of prizes at every level, the same way a scratcher is. The lottery publishes how many prizes are left, and that's what lets us work out what a ticket is worth today.

Does every game have a jackpot that grows?

No. Some games have a progressive jackpot that grows as tickets sell and starts over when somebody wins it. The rest have a fixed top prize. The "Top prize" column marks which is which.

Do games share a jackpot?

No. In Maryland each progressive game has its own jackpot, fed only by its own ticket sales. Each game also has its own pool of tickets, so the odds of winning the top prize are different from game to game.

Is Fast Play a better buy than a scratcher?

It depends on the price. The "Fast Play or a scratcher?" table on this page compares the two at every ticket price, using today's numbers for both.

How is the score figured?

It's the same 0 to 100 SmartScratcher Score the app uses for scratchers. Most of it comes from how much a game pays back per dollar. The rest comes from how many top prizes are left and whether the odds have gotten better or worse since the game started.

How often is this updated?

Twice a day, in the morning and again in the late afternoon. The jackpots keep growing in between, so the amount at the counter may be a little higher.

Is Fast Play in the SmartScratcher app?

Not yet. We're testing it here first, and it's free while we do. The app covers scratch-offs today.
