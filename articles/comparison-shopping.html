YOU'D NEVER BOOK THE FIRST FLIGHT YOU SEE.

So why grab the first scratcher off the rack? Boarding
Carrier · SmartScratcher Date · Jun 16 2026 Boarding · 5 min read

Think about the last trip you booked. You probably opened a couple of tabs. Compared the 6 a.m. flight against the one that doesn't make you set an alarm. Squinted at whether "free cancellation" was worth twenty bucks. Read three hotel reviews to make sure the "ocean view" wasn't a parking lot with a telescope.

You did all that for a trip you take once a year. Nobody books the first result. That's not being cheap — that's just knowing what your money's buying before you hand it over.

Then you stop for gas, walk up to the lottery counter, and point at whatever's shiniest. The big new $20 one with the gold foil. No tabs. No comparing. No idea what you're actually getting.

The same instinct that makes you check three hotels just… switches off at the scratcher rack.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: two scratchers at the same price are not the same deal. Not even close. And the difference is sitting right there in plain sight — you just can't see it from the counter.

Segment 01 THE HIDDEN FARE

When you book a flight, the price at least tells you something. A nonstop at noon usually costs more than a red-eye with a layover — the number climbs as the trip gets better, and you get to decide what the extra's worth. The price is a signal.

Scratchers take that signal away. Every $20 game costs $20, full stop — whether it's loaded with prizes or completely picked clean. The price never moves, so it can't tell you a single thing about which one's actually worth buying.

And the part that does change is invisible from the counter. Every game launches with a fixed number of big prizes, and as people buy and scratch, those get claimed — but the rack never gets restocked or re-priced. So a game whose top prizes are already gone sits there looking brand new, same $20 as the one beside it that's still loaded. It's been picked clean — and there's no sticker on the dispenser to warn you.

That's the entire pitch on two boarding passes. You're spending the twenty bucks regardless — it's your entertainment money and you're allowed to enjoy it. The only question is whether you spend it on the game that still has real prizes left to chase, or the one that's been drained dry while still wearing its launch-day shine.

Segment 03 THE TWO NUMBERS

You don't need to understand the math, the same way you don't need to know how an airline prices a fare to spot the better seat. You just need a couple of honest numbers you can compare.

The big one is the SmartScratcher Score — a single rating from 0 to 100 for every active game. It quietly weighs how many prizes are still out there, the estimated value, jackpot availability, and a few other things, then hands you one number. Empire Gold boards at 88. Skyline Cash limps in at 31. Like sorting hotels by rating instead of reading every review yourself.

Underneath that is estimated value, the plainest-English piece: picture buying every remaining ticket in the game and tallying what comes back, then boiling it down per ticket. On Empire Gold that's around $14.60; on the picked-clean one it's down near $9.40 — same $20 out of your pocket either way. You don't do that math. The app does. You just glance and pick the bigger number.

Segment 04 THE 10-SECOND CHECK

Here's the whole routine. It's shorter than reading this sentence took:

  • Open the app and tap your state.
  • Tap your price point — the $5s, the $10s, whatever you're feeling today.
  • Look at the top of the list. The highest SmartScratcher Scores are already floated up top, and the picked-clean ones are bumped to the bottom.
  • Buy off the top, not off the foil. That's it. You're back in the car before the person behind you finishes their coffee.

That's the same move you already make on every other purchase that matters to you. Check before you buy. Get the best bang for your buck. You're not changing your budget or your habit — you're just aiming the same money at a better game.

Segment 05 THE HONEST PART

Let's be straight, because we always are: a scratch-off is a game of chance. Nothing — no app, no system, no lucky gas station — changes that. Comparing flights doesn't guarantee a smooth trip, and checking a SmartScratcher Score doesn't guarantee a winner.

What it does is stop you from overpaying for an empty game. It's the difference between booking blind and booking informed. You're going to scratch either way — so you might as well know which games still have something worth scratching for.

That's the whole idea. Not a winning system. Just the price-check you already do everywhere else, finally pointed at the rack.

Pre-flight QUICK QUESTIONS
So will this tell me which one wins?

Nope — and anyone who tells you they can is selling something. Scratchers are chance. SmartScratcher just shows you which games still have prizes left to claim, so you don't pay full price for one that's already picked clean.

What's the SmartScratcher Score?

One number from 0 to 100 that ranks every active game. It weighs estimated value, how many prizes are still unclaimed, jackpot availability, and more — so you can compare two games at the same price without doing any math.

And "estimated value"?

Roughly what comes back per ticket if you bought every remaining ticket in a game. Higher means more prize value still on the table at that price. It's one of the inputs behind the Score.

Which states does it cover?

California, Florida, New York, North Carolina, Oklahoma, and Texas. More on the way.

Is it free?

Yes — the free tier shows active games and remaining prizes. Premium ($2.99/month, free trial included) adds the SmartScratcher Score, smart sorting by value, estimated value, and a daily summary of what changed overnight.
